01 · The Opportunity

The asset competitors can't copy

Every heritage house sells lineage, a signature motif, and control over the object's origin story. Cartier, Bulgari, Tiffany, and Van Cleef & Arpels buy colored stones on the open market — none can make a geographic‑origin claim as credible as a house that owns the relationship with Ratnapura miners and in‑house cutters. Ceylon‑origin sapphires already carry a real market premium when backed by a lab origin report; right now dealers and auction houses capture that premium. A vertically integrated brand can capture it directly and build the whole identity around it.

The differentiator: Graff‑style vertical control (mine to masterpiece) combined with lab‑verified Ceylon provenance on every signature piece — a claim no reference brand studied can make for colored stones.

02 · Brand Strategy

Verifiable provenance

03 · What the Research Says

UX & conversion patterns

Full detail in docs/02-ux-design-patterns.md. Luxury jewellery converts far below general e‑commerce because customers research across multiple visits — design for trust built over time, not a single fast checkout.

Product pages

  • 360° spin (drag, not autoplay), macro zoom on prongs and hallmarks, as‑worn lifestyle shots.
  • Certification (GIA/GRS/AGL) shown on‑page, beside the price — not linked off‑site.

Trust & conversion

  • Catalog pricing for bridal/fine jewellery; "Price on Request" reserved for one‑of‑a‑kind pieces behind an advisor.
  • A persistent "Book a Video Consultation" CTA on high‑value pages — the single highest‑leverage mechanic found.

AR & configurators

  • Fewer returns (25–40%) and longer engagement with AR try‑on; ship strong 360° photography first, AR in Phase 2.
  • Even category leaders (James Allen, Blue Nile) still mail physical ring sizers — camera sizing remains an open opportunity.

Retention

  • Access and recognition, not discounts — private‑sale invites outperform points programs at this tier.
  • Care reminders and anniversary triggers are the highest‑converting lifecycle emails in the category.

Mobile

  • Mobile drives discovery; desktop and advisor‑assisted channels close the sale. Cart and wishlist must persist across devices.

Checkout

  • Guest checkout stays available at every phase; itemized duty/tax shown upfront for international orders.
  • Financing (Affirm/Klarna) is table stakes at this price point once international volume justifies it.

04 · Technical Architecture

Platform & systems

Full detail in docs/03-technical-architecture.md. Shopify Plus + Hydrogen (Next.js) now — fast to market, PCI scope mostly handled — with a custom PIM layer to work around Shopify's variant limits for metal/stone/size configurability. Move to composable commerce (commercetools) only once genuinely outgrowing it.

The hard problem: Stripe, Adyen, and PayPal don't onboard Sri Lanka–domiciled merchants directly. The standard route is a foreign holding entity (US, UK, Singapore, or UAE) for the international merchant account, paired with PayHere/WebXPay for local LKR traffic — routine for Sri Lankan exporters, but a real legal step, not just an engineering one.

05 · Roadmap

Sequenced build plan

Full detail in docs/04-roadmap.md. Each phase is justified by evidence from the one before it, not built speculatively.

Phase 0 · Weeks 1–4

Foundation

  • Lock name, motif, identity; clear trademark and domain.
  • Incorporate the foreign entity for global payments; open local PayHere/WebXPay account.
  • Photography direction and story‑page IA locked.
Phase 1 · Months 2–4

MVP Launch

  • Shopify Plus + Hydrogen storefront live; 360°/zoom PDPs with on‑page certification.
  • Guest checkout, Stripe + local rail both live, itemized international duty.
  • WhatsApp/video consultation booking on every high‑value page — not deferred.
Phase 2 · Months 4–8

Growth

  • AR try‑on and metal/stone/size configurator; mailed ring‑sizer fallback.
  • Clienteling CRM, private‑sale access, lifecycle care/anniversary email‑SMS.
  • Financing at checkout; multi‑currency pricing.
Phase 3 · Months 8–14

Scale

  • NFC tag + certificate lookup page for signature pieces.
  • Personalization engine; Gulf market financing (Tabby/Tamara) if warranted.
  • Migrate PIM/commerce only if genuinely outgrowing Shopify Plus.
Phase 4 · Year 2+

Global Prestige

  • High Jewellery private‑sale tier, by‑appointment, POR pricing.
  • RJC certification decision; flagship boutique and PR strategy.

Deliberately not building first

  • No bespoke blockchain — NFC + a certificate lookup page carries the trust value at a fraction of the cost.
  • No AR/configurator before MVP traction — strong photography carries Phase 1.
  • No loyalty‑points/discount program, at any phase — it undercuts luxury positioning.
  • No forced account creation at checkout, ever — guest checkout stays available.

This is a working draft, not a final decision. Working name: Cassian, pending formal trademark clearance. Open items: the motif, RJC certification path, and whether the founder/family story is used publicly.
